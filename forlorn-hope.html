The Forlorn Hope

The knightly order and standing guard of Temporis.

← Return to the civic portal

The city stands behind them

What We Are

The Forlorn Hope are the guardians of Temporis. Not lords, not blood-borne rulers, and not a reward for birth. They are a duty that any resident may be called to serve, and no one is owed the honor of standing in the line.

Our name comes from the knights who stood upon the surface of a dying world while humanity sheltered beneath it. They held the line because someone had to, and the same principle remains true in Temporis. The wall is not a symbol. It is a promise that danger may be met before it reaches the weak.

"While one stands, hope remains."

Our Three Duties

Protect

Protect the weak and stand between danger and those who would suffer from it.

Preserve

Preserve knowledge, records, techniques, names, and lives from needless loss.

Endure

Remain steady when fear, danger, or uncertainty would make standing difficult.

Service in the Hope

The Hope serves across the walls, roads, wards, expeditions, and archives of Temporis. Squires and Knights may be assigned to gates, patrols, escorts, emergency response, archive security, or long-range missions beyond the city. There is no lesser posting. A Squire at a gate can hold as much of the line as a Knight who never learns restraint.

The order is organized into Squires, Knights, and Captains, each divided into three ranks. Rank measures trust and responsibility, not merely combat ability. The full rank structure, oath, and teachings of the order are kept in the Forlorn Hope Manual.

Squires

New members learn discipline, watchfulness, and service under supervision before they are trusted with larger burdens.

Knight

Experienced defenders entrusted with command of smaller detachments, difficult escorts, and emergency response in open danger.

Captains

Senior commanders whose judgment decides where the line is held, where it is withdrawn, and what must not be lost.

Standards and Conduct

A member of the Hope is expected to be steady in judgment, disciplined in action, and honest in both success and failure. The order does not reward vanity, spectacle, or the pride that thinks danger exists to prove a person is brave. It rewards preparation, reliability, and the willingness to stand when others are afraid.

  • Discipline is not obedience for obedience's sake. It is the means by which the order remains useful when fear is loud.
  • Knowledge is protected as fiercely as walls. Records, names, and techniques are not discarded lightly.
  • Service does not end when the task becomes unpleasant. A watchman is judged by the hour he remains at his post, not by the moment he feels heroic.

Interested in Serving?

Petitions for service begin in character. The full expectations, rank structure, oath, duties, and teachings of the order are maintained by the Department of the Forlorn Hope.
