The Forlorn Hope

The modern order of Temporis, born from a dying world and shaped for the task that remains.

← Return to City Departments

The city stands behind them

What We Are

The Forlorn Hope are the guardians of Temporis. Not lords, not blood-born rulers, and not a reward for birth. They are a duty that any resident may be called to serve, and no one is owed the honor of standing in the line.

They inherit the name of those who once stood on the surface of a dying world while the rest of humanity sheltered underground. The old order sought transcendence. The modern one does not. It remembers the courage, rejects the obsession, and keeps the part that matters: someone had to stand there.

"Protect. Preserve. Endure."

The original Forlorn Hope

The older order emerged in the last age of the Rot, when humanity's final struggle was not merely survival but whether the work of the Great Machine and the lives it sheltered would endure. They were not simple soldiers. They were religious, armored defenders with an almost mystical interpretation of Jonas Falx.

  • They believed the Great Machine was more than a machine. It was a spiritual and civil promise.
  • They sought ascent, contact with the Prima Materia, and a direct relation to the world beyond the known one.
  • They remained on the surface while much of humanity fled beneath it.
  • They stood between desperate survivors and the last refuge of the living, holding a line that could not be withdrawn.

That image is dramatic, even legendary: mounted knights standing on a dying world while the desperate struggled for entry behind them. Their story ends in silence and uncertainty, and that uncertainty is important. Whether they died, ascended, or became something else, the modern order inherited the name and the legend without inheriting the obsession that shaped it.

The modern order

The modern Forlorn Hope is Temporis's military order, city guard, expeditionary force, emergency service, and guardian of what has been rebuilt. It is the same old name, but the philosophy is different. The modern order does not ask whether it can become more than human. It asks what must remain standing when fear and danger arrive.

Protect the Weak

Stand between danger and those who would otherwise suffer from it.

Be the Wall

Hold the line when there is still something worth defending.

Preserve Knowledge

Protect records, techniques, names, and wisdom from needless loss.

That last duty is central to Temporis. The old order protected humanity's final hope. The modern order protects what humanity has rebuilt, and that includes the city's memory, archives, and future.

Ascension versus endurance

The sharpest difference is philosophical. The original order looked toward ascension, transcendence, and becoming more than mortal. The modern order rejects that mindset. It keeps the courage while discarding the romantic fixation on heroic death.

The old Forlorn Hope asked, "What might humanity become?" The modern one asks, "Who will still be here tomorrow?" That is the heart of the shift.

The old order sought to become something greater than human. The modern order seeks to be worthy of the people it protects.

Death is not a noble excuse

The ancient image is seductive: a mounted knight standing alone against impossible odds, dying with dignity. The modern order rejects that romanticism. It does not admire avoidable sacrifice. A Squire who survives and protects someone is worth more than a Knight who falls gloriously for a battle that never had to be chosen.

Seraphim may return after death, and that is not a license for recklessness. Return is possible, therefore it must never become an excuse to gamble the lives of others.

Not a bloodline. Not a reward.

The order is deliberately open in a way the old one was not. It does not demand noble birth, hereditary prestige, or a religious pedigree. It admits that service is a duty, not a reward. Rank is measured in responsibility and trust, not in lineage or spectacle.

Squires

Learn discipline, watchfulness, and service before greater trust is placed in their hands.

Knight

Holds the blade in danger and carries decisions beyond simple combat.

Captains

Make the choices that determine who is saved, who holds the line, and what cannot be lost.

A historical restoration, not a direct continuation

The Forlorn Hope is not a revival of the old order's theology. It is a civic and martial institution shaped by that history while rejecting what it most dangerously idealized. It studies the old knights, learns from them, and keeps what was worth keeping: courage, discipline, and the will to stand at the wall when the alternative is fear on a wider scale.

It is the difference between a force that sought transcendence and a force that chose endurance. In Temporis, endurance is the more honest thing.
